# Section A: Short Answer Questions

## 1. Define learner autonomy.

### Model Answer Points

Learner autonomy means the learner’s **capacity or ability to take control of their own learning**. It includes taking responsibility for decisions about learning goals, content, methods, monitoring progress, and evaluating outcomes.

A strong answer should mention:

* control over learning;
* responsibility;
* decision-making;
* planning, monitoring, and evaluation;
* not simply studying alone.

---

## 2. What does Holec mean by “taking charge of one’s own learning”?

### Model Answer Points

Holec means that learners are responsible for decisions about all aspects of their learning.

They should be able to:

1. determine objectives;
2. define content and progression;
3. select methods and techniques;
4. monitor the process of acquisition;
5. evaluate what has been acquired.

This shows that autonomy is not only about independence, but about **conscious responsibility and decision-making**.

---

## 3. Explain Freire’s “banking model” of education.

### Model Answer Points

Freire’s banking model describes traditional education where learners are treated like **containers** and teachers “deposit” knowledge into them.

In this model:

* the teacher controls knowledge;
* students receive and store information;
* learners are passive;
* learning becomes memorization;
* students are not encouraged to question or think critically.

Freire criticized this because learning should be something learners **do**, not something done **to** them.

---

## 4. How does Illich’s criticism of education support learner autonomy?

### Model Answer Points

Illich criticized the dependency created by educational systems. He argued that schools often make learners believe that:

* only teachers can teach;
* only institutions are places of learning;
* learners cannot learn without formal systems.

This supports learner autonomy because Illich encouraged:

* self-learning;
* liberation of education;
* preservation of autonomy;
* learning outside formal institutions.

---

## 5. What is meant by “autonomy links freedom and responsibility”?

### Model Answer Points

Autonomy gives learners freedom to make choices about their learning, but this freedom must be connected to responsibility.

This means learners should:

* make choices;
* accept consequences;
* monitor their progress;
* evaluate their learning;
* not expect the teacher to control everything.

Autonomy is not “doing whatever you want.” It is **responsible freedom**.

# Section B: Comparison Questions

## 6. Differentiate between learner autonomy and individualization.

### Model Answer Points

**Individualization** means learning is adapted to the learner’s level, needs, or pace by the teacher or system.

**Learner autonomy** means the learner takes responsibility and makes decisions about learning.

| Individualization                   | Learner Autonomy                      |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Learning is adapted for the learner | Learning is controlled by the learner |
| Teacher/system usually decides      | Learner makes meaningful decisions    |
| Supports learner needs              | Requires responsibility and control   |
| May still be teacher-led            | Requires learner participation        |

Example: If a teacher gives one student basic vocabulary and another student advanced idioms, this is individualization, not necessarily autonomy.

---

## 7. Differentiate between self-instruction and learner autonomy.

### Model Answer Points

**Self-instruction** means learning without the direct control or physical presence of a teacher.

**Learner autonomy** means taking responsibility for learning decisions.

A learner may study alone but still follow a plan created by someone else. Therefore, self-instruction does not automatically mean autonomy.

A strong answer should say:

* self-instruction is about the **setting** or absence of teacher control;
* autonomy is about **capacity, choice, responsibility, and reflection**.

---

## 8. Differentiate between independent learning and learner autonomy.

### Model Answer Points

**Independent learning** means working alone on a task.

**Learner autonomy** means consciously taking control of learning.

A student may work alone because the teacher told them to complete a worksheet quietly. This is independent work, but not necessarily autonomy because the learner may have:

* no choice;
* no control;
* no personal goal;
* no role in evaluation.

Therefore, working alone does not equal being autonomous.

---

## 9. Compare self-direction and self-regulation.

### Model Answer Points

**Self-direction** is an attitude where the learner accepts responsibility for learning.

**Self-regulation** involves managing the learning process psychologically.

| Self-Direction                   | Self-Regulation                                       |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Attitude toward learning         | Process of controlling learning                       |
| Learner accepts responsibility   | Learner manages motivation, attention, and strategies |
| May still rely on teacher action | Includes planning, monitoring, and evaluating         |
| Close to autonomy                | Very central to autonomy                              |

Example: A student asking the teacher for pronunciation materials shows self-direction. A student planning, monitoring, and evaluating pronunciation practice shows self-regulation.

---

## 10. Explain the difference between self-access and learner autonomy.

### Model Answer Points

**Self-access** means learners have access to learning materials or spaces, such as a resource room, website, or online platform.

**Learner autonomy** means the learner has the skill and willingness to use those materials effectively.

Self-access provides:

* tools;
* resources;
* opportunity.

Autonomy requires:

* goals;
* motivation;
* strategy;
* reflection;
* evaluation.

Having resources does not automatically create autonomy.

# Section C: Conceptual Questions

## 11. Explain Little’s five misconceptions about learner autonomy.

### Model Answer Points

Little identifies five common misconceptions:

1. **Autonomy means self-instruction**
   Incorrect because autonomy is not just learning without a teacher.

2. **Teachers must give up all control**
   Incorrect because autonomy can happen in structured classrooms.

3. **Autonomy is a new methodology**
   Incorrect because autonomy is not a fixed teaching method.

4. **Autonomy is one simple behavior**
   Incorrect because it takes different forms depending on age, level, context, and goals.

5. **Autonomy is permanent**
   Incorrect because autonomy can change depending on situation, task, motivation, and environment.

---

## 12. Why is learner autonomy not a permanent state?

### Model Answer Points

Learner autonomy is not permanent because it can fluctuate.

A learner may be autonomous:

* in one skill but not another;
* in one task but not another;
* when motivated but not when tired;
* in a supportive environment but not in a restrictive one.

Autonomy may change depending on:

* mood;
* task difficulty;
* confidence;
* motivation;
* environment;
* language competence.

---

## 13. Explain the social dimension of learner autonomy.

### Model Answer Points

The social dimension means that autonomy does not mean isolation. Learners can develop autonomy through interaction and collaboration.

Autonomous learning may involve:

* peers;
* teachers;
* group work;
* discussion;
* collaboration;
* social support.

This connects with Vygotsky’s Zone of Proximal Development because support from others can help learners become more independent.

---

## 14. Explain the political dimension of learner autonomy.

### Model Answer Points

The political dimension is about power, control, and decision-making in education.

It asks questions such as:

* Who chooses the learning materials?
* Who decides the goals?
* Who controls assessment?
* Who decides what counts as success?

This dimension shows that autonomy is affected by institutions, policies, curricula, and exams, not only by learner motivation.

# Section D: Learners’ Roles

## 15. What are learners’ roles in developing autonomy?

### Model Answer Points

Learners contribute to autonomy through:

1. their motivation or will to act;
2. the actions they choose to take.

Learners need to:

* accept responsibility;
* understand their learning needs;
* use strategies;
* evaluate materials;
* reflect on progress;
* use peers and others as resources.

Learners are not simply recipients of education. They are active participants in the learning process.

---

## 16. How can learners’ beliefs restrict autonomy?

### Model Answer Points

Learners’ beliefs can restrict autonomy if they believe that:

* only teachers can explain everything;
* language learning is only grammar memorization;
* correct language means only native-like language;
* mistakes are unacceptable;
* learning only happens in classrooms.

These beliefs can make learners passive, dependent, anxious, or unwilling to take initiative.

---

## 17. According to Sturtridge, how can learners minimize constraints on autonomy?

### Model Answer Points

Learners can be trained to minimize constraints by learning how to:

1. recognize unsuitable materials;
2. use materials productively;
3. be aware of different types of materials;
4. understand the purpose of tasks;
5. evaluate tasks;
6. use peers and others as resources.

This helps learners become more capable of managing their own learning.

---

## 18. Explain the role of learning strategies in learner autonomy.

### Model Answer Points

Learning strategies help learners control and improve their learning.

O’Malley and Chamot classify strategies into:

1. **Socio-affective strategies**

   * managing emotions and using social support.

2. **Cognitive strategies**

   * using mental skills to process language.

3. **Metacognitive strategies**

   * planning, monitoring, and evaluating learning.

Metacognitive strategies are especially important because autonomy requires learners to think about and control their learning process.

# Section E: Teachers’ Roles

## 19. How can teachers promote learner autonomy?

### Model Answer Points

Teachers can promote autonomy by:

* giving meaningful choices;
* encouraging reflection;
* training learning strategies;
* allowing goal-setting;
* supporting peer collaboration;
* connecting tasks to real life;
* helping learners evaluate progress;
* reducing unnecessary control;
* holding learners accountable.

Teachers do not disappear in autonomy. They guide and support learners.

---

## 20. How can teachers hinder learner autonomy?

### Model Answer Points

Teachers may hinder autonomy when they:

* spoon-feed students;
* control every decision;
* focus only on exams;
* discourage questions;
* treat learners as passive;
* give no opportunity for choice;
* prevent reflection;
* make students dependent on teacher answers.

This keeps learners in a passive role.

---

## 21. Explain Breen and Mann’s view of teachers of autonomous learners.

### Model Answer Points

Breen and Mann argue that teachers should:

1. know their own beliefs about teaching and learning;
2. reflect on how their decisions affect each learner;
3. assume each learner is capable of taking an autonomous stance;
4. adapt their practice to foster learner autonomy.

This means teachers need to be reflective, flexible, and supportive.

---

## 22. According to Oxford, what qualities do learners appreciate in teachers?

### Model Answer Points

Learners appreciate teachers who show:

* interest;
* enthusiasm;
* clarity;
* stimulation of curiosity;
* concern;
* availability;
* obvious structure.

This shows that autonomy does not remove the teacher’s importance. Learners still need structure, support, and motivation.

# Section F: Self-Determination Theory

## 23. Explain the connection between Self-Determination Theory and learner autonomy.

### Model Answer Points

Self-Determination Theory explains motivation through three basic needs:

1. **Autonomy**

   * feeling in control of one’s goals and actions.

2. **Competence**

   * feeling able to master tasks and develop skills.

3. **Relatedness**

   * feeling connected to and supported by others.

When teachers give learners choices, learners feel more autonomous. This increases motivation, engagement, independence, and deeper learning.

---

## 24. Does Self-Determination Theory reject accountability? Explain.

### Model Answer Points

No, SDT does not reject accountability.

It supports accountability when combined with:

* autonomy;
* competence;
* relatedness.

This means students can still be responsible for learning outcomes while also having meaningful choices and support.

Autonomy does not mean no rules or no responsibility.

---

# Section G: Institutional Role

## 25. Explain how institutions can restrict learner autonomy.

### Model Answer Points

Institutions can restrict autonomy through:

* national education policies;
* institutional regulations;
* fixed curricula;
* external exams;
* classroom organization;
* class size;
* limited resources;
* dominant beliefs about language teaching.

External exams may make teachers and learners focus only on fixed outcomes, reducing learner choice.

---

## 26. Explain Benson’s sources of constraint on learner autonomy.

### Model Answer Points

Benson identifies four main sources of constraint:

1. **Policy constraints**

   * national education policies.

2. **Institutional constraints**

   * rules and regulations.

3. **Physical and social organization of the institution**

   * classroom layout, class size, schedules, resources, hierarchy.

4. **Dominant conceptions of language and methodology**

   * beliefs about what language is and how it should be taught.

---

## 27. What is the role of senior administrators in learner autonomy?

### Model Answer Points

Senior administrators create the environment where teachers and learners work.

They must work within:

* legislation;
* institutional objectives;
* evaluation criteria;
* local and national stakeholder expectations.

They can either support autonomy by creating flexible, supportive environments or restrict autonomy through rigid policies and assessment systems.

# Section H: Application / Scenario Questions

## 28. A teacher gives students three topics to choose from for a speaking task and asks them to reflect on their performance afterward. Explain how this supports autonomy.

### Model Answer Points

This supports autonomy because students are given choice and reflection.

The teacher supports:

* autonomy through topic choice;
* self-regulation through reflection;
* responsibility through self-evaluation;
* motivation through meaningful choice.

This also connects to SDT because choice can increase autonomy, motivation, engagement, and deeper learning.

## 29. A student studies English alone every day using a fixed online course, but never sets goals, monitors progress, or evaluates learning. Is this learner autonomous?

### Model Answer Points

Not fully.

The student is practicing **self-instruction** because they study without direct teacher control. However, this is not full autonomy because they do not:

* set goals;
* choose methods;
* monitor progress;
* evaluate learning;
* adjust strategies.

Studying alone does not automatically mean autonomy.

---

## 30. A school provides a digital self-access platform, but students rarely use it effectively. Explain why self-access alone is not enough.

### Model Answer Points

Self-access provides tools and opportunities, but autonomy requires the skill and will to use those tools effectively.

Students may need training in:

* setting goals;
* choosing suitable materials;
* using resources productively;
* monitoring progress;
* evaluating tasks.

Therefore, resources alone do not create autonomous learners.

## 32. Compare learner autonomy with related concepts such as self-instruction, independent learning, self-direction, self-regulation, individualization, and self-access.

### Model Answer Structure

**Introduction**

* State that these concepts are related but not identical.

**Body**

Explain each:

* individualization = learning adapted for learner;
* self-instruction = learning without direct teacher control;
* self-direction = accepting responsibility;
* independent learning = working alone;
* self-regulation = managing motivation, attention, and strategies;
* self-access = access to resources.

**Main argument**

The key difference is **control**.

Autonomy requires conscious decision-making, responsibility, planning, monitoring, and evaluation.

**Conclusion**

These concepts can support autonomy, but none of them alone equals full learner autonomy.

## 34. Explain how Self-Determination Theory supports learner autonomy.

### Model Answer Structure

**Introduction**

* SDT explains motivation and psychological needs.

**Body**

Explain the three needs:

1. autonomy;
2. competence;
3. relatedness.

Then explain the chain:

**Teacher gives choices → learner feels autonomous → motivation increases → engagement improves → deeper learning develops**

**Important point**

SDT does not reject accountability. It supports responsible learning with autonomy, competence, and relatedness.

**Conclusion**

SDT supports learner autonomy because it explains how choice, support, and competence increase motivation and independence.

## 35. Discuss the objections to learner autonomy.

### Model Answer Structure

**Introduction**

* Learner autonomy is important, but it has objections and limitations.

**Objection 1: Free will**

* Some question whether learners can truly choose freely under curricula, exams, policies, and social pressure.

**Objection 2: Valid educational goal**

* Some question whether autonomy should always be a goal, especially when learners need guidance.

**Objection 3: Cultural limitations**

* Some cultures may expect stronger teacher authority.
* Learner choice may be unfamiliar or uncomfortable.

**Additional concerns**

* learner choice may harm learning if unsupported;
* teacher support is still needed;
* autonomy should be balanced with accountability.

**Conclusion**

Autonomy is valuable, but it must be adapted to context, culture, learner readiness, and institutional realities.
