# Section C: Compare and Contrast Questions

## 1. Compare information literacy and media literacy.

**Model Answer:**
Information literacy focuses on finding, evaluating, and using information from sources. It deals with credibility, accuracy, fake news, facts, opinions, and synthesis. Media literacy focuses on analysing media messages. It deals with bias, tone, agenda, persuasive language, and the purpose behind media content. Both skills help students become critical users of information, but information literacy focuses more on sources while media literacy focuses more on messages.

---

## 2. Compare collaboration and communication.

**Model Answer:**
Collaboration is working effectively and respectfully with others to achieve a common goal. It involves shared responsibility, compromise, and building on others’ ideas. Communication is expressing ideas clearly and effectively through speaking, writing, listening, and nonverbal forms. Collaboration often requires communication, but they are not the same. Collaboration focuses on teamwork, while communication focuses on exchanging meaning clearly.

---

## 3. Compare traditional teaching and modern teaching.

**Model Answer:**
Traditional teaching views the teacher as the main source of information. Students are often passive listeners, and learning focuses on memorisation and summative tests. Modern teaching views the teacher as a learning facilitator. The teacher designs engaging learning experiences, guides inquiry, supports self-directed projects, gives continuous feedback, and encourages reflection. The modern approach better supports 21st century skills because students actively practise thinking, creating, collaborating, and solving problems.

---

## 4. Compare creativity and critical thinking.

**Model Answer:**
Creativity is about producing new and valuable ideas by thinking in new ways. It includes innovation, synthesis, and thinking outside the box. Critical thinking is about analysing, evaluating, questioning assumptions, and judging ideas using logic and evidence. Creativity generates ideas, while critical thinking improves and tests them. Both are needed because new ideas must also be logical and useful.

---

## 5. Compare flexibility and productivity.

**Model Answer:**
Flexibility is the ability to adapt to change and respond to new challenges. Productivity is the ability to manage time, tasks, and energy effectively to achieve goals. Flexibility helps students adjust when situations change, while productivity helps them stay organised and complete tasks successfully. Both are important for personal and professional success.

# Section D: Application Questions

## 1. Design a short English activity that develops all 4Cs.

**Model Answer:**

### Activity: Create a Short Story Together

Students work in groups to design and present a short story.

* **Creativity:** Students brainstorm plot ideas, characters, and endings.
* **Critical Thinking:** Students discuss which ideas fit best and choose the strongest storyline.
* **Collaboration:** Students divide roles and write the story together while sharing responsibility.
* **Communication:** Students present the final story clearly to the class.

This activity develops all 4Cs because students generate ideas, evaluate them, work as a team, and present their final product.

---

## 2. Design an activity that develops information literacy, media literacy, and technology literacy.

**Model Answer:**

### Activity: Evaluate Online News

Students find two online articles about the same event.

* **Information Literacy:** Students identify facts and opinions and check whether the sources are credible.
* **Media Literacy:** Students analyse tone, bias, purpose, and possible agenda.
* **Technology Literacy:** Students use Google Slides to present their findings responsibly.

This activity develops literacy skills because students research, evaluate, analyse, synthesise, and present information using digital tools.

---

## 3. Give an example of how a teacher can develop flexibility in students.

**Model Answer:**
A teacher can develop flexibility by giving students a project and then changing one requirement halfway through. Students must adjust their plan, discuss new options, and continue working. The teacher can also use role-play, reflection on feedback, and varied assessment formats to help students practise adapting to change.

---

## 4. Give an example of how a teacher can develop initiative.

**Model Answer:**
A teacher can give students a choice-based project where they select their own topic, plan their steps, choose resources, and present their final work. This develops initiative because students take responsibility and act independently instead of waiting for every instruction.

---

## 5. How can productivity be developed in the classroom?

**Model Answer:**
Productivity can be developed through time management training, task trackers, goal-setting exercises, project deadlines, and workload planning. Students can learn to prioritise tasks, manage their energy, and complete work on time.

# Section E: Long Essay Questions

## 1. Explain the importance of 21st century skills in education.

**Model Answer:**
21st century skills are important because the modern world is changing quickly. In the past, people often had one job for life, but today’s students may have many careers and may work in jobs that do not yet exist. Also, information is no longer difficult to find because students can search for facts online in seconds. Therefore, education should not focus only on memorisation.

Students need skills that help them use knowledge effectively. Learning skills such as critical thinking, creativity, collaboration, and communication help students solve problems, create ideas, work with others, and express themselves clearly. Literacy skills help students evaluate information, understand media bias, and use technology responsibly. Life skills help students adapt to change, show leadership, take initiative, manage time, and interact respectfully with others.

Overall, 21st century skills prepare learners to become adaptable, valuable, successful, and future-ready. They help students succeed not only in school but also in careers and personal life.

---

## 2. Explain the 4Cs and give an example for each one.

**Model Answer:**
The 4Cs are critical thinking, creativity, collaboration, and communication. They are learning skills that help students think deeply and work effectively with others.

Critical thinking is the ability to analyse facts, evaluate arguments, question assumptions, draw inferences, and solve problems. An example is comparing two news articles to identify bias.

Creativity is the ability to produce new and valuable ideas by thinking in new ways. It is not only about art. An example is writing an alternative ending to a story.

Collaboration is working effectively and respectfully with others to achieve a common goal. It is not just dividing work. An example is completing a group project where students share responsibility and build on each other’s ideas.

Communication is expressing thoughts clearly and effectively in oral, written, and nonverbal forms. It also includes active listening and choosing the right tool for the audience. An example is giving an oral presentation or participating in a class debate.

Together, the 4Cs help students become active, creative, thoughtful, and effective learners.

---

## 3. Explain the difference between the three literacy skills.

**Model Answer:**
The three literacy skills are information literacy, media literacy, and technology literacy. These skills help students deal with information and digital content in today’s world.

Information literacy is the ability to find, evaluate, and use information effectively, responsibly, and safely. It includes finding credible sources, identifying fake news, evaluating bias, and synthesising information from different sources.

Media literacy is the ability to access, analyse, evaluate, and create media messages in different forms. It focuses on understanding bias, tone, persuasive language, agenda, and the purpose behind media content. For example, students may analyse advertisements to understand how language is used to persuade people.

Technology literacy is the ability to use technology tools effectively to access, manage, integrate, evaluate, and create information. It includes using tools such as Google Slides, Padlet, digital poster software, and coding platforms for learning and collaboration.

Although the three skills are connected, each one has a different focus. Information literacy focuses on the quality and use of information, media literacy focuses on messages and bias, and technology literacy focuses on using digital tools effectively and responsibly.

---

## 4. Explain the five life skills and their importance.

**Model Answer:**
Life skills are personal and professional abilities that help individuals adapt to the changing world and succeed in their careers and personal lives. The five life skills are flexibility, leadership, initiative, productivity, and social skills.

Flexibility is the ability to adapt to change and new challenges. It is important because students will face unexpected situations in school, work, and life.

Leadership is the ability to guide, inspire, and influence others to achieve common goals. It helps students take responsibility and support group success.

Initiative is the ability to take responsibility and act independently. It is important because students should not always wait for instructions; they should be proactive and responsible for their learning.

Productivity is managing time, tasks, and energy effectively to achieve goals. It helps students meet deadlines, manage workloads, and set academic goals.

Social skills are the ability to interact effectively with others while showing empathy and respect. They help students build positive relationships, resolve conflicts, and understand different perspectives.

Together, these life skills prepare students for success in school, work, and personal life.

---

## 5. Explain how teachers can implement 21st century skills in the curriculum.

**Model Answer:**
Teachers can implement 21st century skills by designing learning experiences that allow students to apply knowledge and practise real-world skills. One way is through project-based learning. Instead of giving isolated worksheets, teachers can ask students to solve complex, real-world problems or create meaningful projects.

Another way is through technology-rich lessons. Technology should not be used only as a decoration or novelty. It should be used as an essential tool for research, collaboration, creation, and communication.

Teachers can also use interdisciplinary learning. This means breaking down subject boundaries and connecting skills from different areas. For example, an English lesson can include ICT, media analysis, critical thinking, and presentation skills.

Finally, teachers can use performance task assessment. Instead of only testing what students can memorise, teachers assess what students can construct, design, solve, or create. This helps students demonstrate practical understanding.

Overall, 21st century skills should be embedded into teaching, learning, and assessment, not taught separately as isolated topics.

### Activity: Compare Two Online Opinions

**English objective:**
Students practise speaking and giving opinions.

**Procedure:**

**Literacy Skill:**

**Life Skill:**
Social skills because students discuss respectfully and listen to different opinions.
